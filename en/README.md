## Intro
My name is Dima Varabei. I'm the organizer of RS School and a Senior Engineering Manager at EPAM Systems.
Historically, EPAM has been hiring RS School graduates for years and continues to do so — but not yet for the so-called "AI Native / AI Factory" projects. I want to change that.

EPAM now has a category of projects where practically 100% of the code is written by AI, while the engineering team owns the delivery process and the result.
The projects themselves are fairly standard web applications for business, built with the familiar JavaScript, .NET, or Java.
One of the key differences is that the entire development process (from ticket to PR) is done by coding agents like Claude Code / Codex / Kimi / Cursor following a special protocol/process (I'll call it AI Factory), with checkpoints where key steps are approved by engineers.
Not to be confused with AI Dark Factory (where this process runs without human control).

The term "open Junior position" isn't quite accurate for the "AI Native / AI Factory" projects mentioned above.
There is no open recruitment the way there used to be. There are thousands of juniors on the market, everyone looks equally impressive on paper, a one-hour technical interview doesn't show the real picture and real knowledge, and team members simply don't have the time or the desire to go through that flow of candidates — it's easier to take mid-level or senior engineers who already work at EPAM.

On the other hand, over the years RS School has built up reputation and trust, so we agreed with the projects that I find suitable candidates and bring them to the project's entry filter.
To start, it will be five people, and if the first five succeed, we'll take more people from more countries, since there are many projects working by the AI Factory process.

Since I've always been an optimist, I hope these 5 people will successfully get hired, and for RS School this will be a good reason to build a course on AI-assisted development, based on the specific project requirements, the development process, and real employment opportunities.

## How the selection works
The selection for the projects has several steps, and the path is the same for everyone (both for EPAM employees who haven't worked by the AI Factory process before, and for external candidates).

1. **A test task from me** — [already published in this repository](../test-task/README.md). This is the bulk of the whole journey: 2–3 weeks on an intentionally imperfect project with problems typical of real codebases. What's tested is not the ability to write from scratch, but the ability to find your way around someone else's code and validate what the agent produces; the final tasks follow a spec-driven process (superpowers). The test-task period is unpaid.
2. **An interview** based on the test-task results.
3. **Joining EPAM as a student**, if you're not already in one of EPAM's learning labs (JS, Java, or .NET). The student status means access to EPAM's infrastructure and to EPAM's version of the factory: a process very similar to the superpowers flow from the test task, with extra stages and small differences. There is no salary at this status.
4. **The final week**: a couple more tasks, now following the factory process on EPAM's tools. If all goes well — consideration for a specific project. Project stacks are usually mixed, like React or Angular plus .NET or Java. Sometimes Full Stack JS.

## Requirements

- Residence in one of these countries: Georgia, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Armenia. The first 5 hires are from these locations only. Going forward, I hope hiring will open up for residents of Belarus, Ukraine, or Poland.
- English — solid B1, ideally B2.
- Solid knowledge of one of the domains: JavaScript/TypeScript, .NET, or Java. On the projects themselves, the frontend is currently React or Angular; the backend is .NET, Java, or Node.js.
- Understanding of how a full-stack application works end-to-end. This doesn't mean you can write everything by hand from scratch — it means you know the fundamentals and are ready to learn fast.
- Engineering basics: Git, CI/CD, task trackers (Jira or similar), a general idea of why cloud providers (AWS, GCP, Azure) exist.
- A paid subscription to a coding agent (Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot, or similar). In 2026 this is a basic requirement — without it you can't get the experience you need. We can't help with the subscription at this stage and expect you to already have one.
- AI basics: understanding what an LLM and a harness are, plus hands-on experience with a coding agent.
